# Penny Break — pattern sheet

> **Short** the failed break of a level : the stock pokes **no more than ~1 %** above a top or a bounce
> level, then gets slammed back under it. A **pattern of its own** when a stock extends into a level
> and fails there, and an **entry signal** inside the other setups — there it says *when* to enter a
> trade the setup already justifies (see [`execution-signals.md`](../notes/execution-signals.md)).

*Last revised : 2026-09-25, from the Trading Desk session of 14 September, « Les Patterns ».*

---

## Entry checklist

| # | Criterion | Value | Why |
|---|-----------|-------|-----|
| 1 | **Chart** | 5-second | On the 1-minute the break-then-slam is invisible. |
| 2 | **A setup around it** | GUS, double top, short into resistance, or a big extension into a level | Penny breaks are everywhere ; alone they are noise. |
| 3 | **A level** | A top, or a bounce level the stock tested several times | The break has to fail at something. |
| 4 | **Break size** | ≤ ~1 % above the level | On an $8 stock, 8 cents. Past that it is a real breakout, not a failed one. |
| 5 | **Rejection** | Right after the break | Enter on it — sometimes after a small bounce. |
| 6 | **Risk** | Usually 2-5 %, stop at the high of the break | Inside a GUS, keep the wide 10-15 % stop planned for it : the signal only places a better entry. |

---

## Why it works

A long waiting for the breakout buys the break. When it fails at once, they give up and sell — the
same selling a double top gets on its retest (see the four sellers in
[`four-sellers.md`](../notes/four-sellers.md)). A triple top is where a long most expects the breakout, so a
triple-top break that dies right away shakes them out hardest.

It is also the way in when a stock extends into a level but never gives the 17-20 % rejection a
[double top](DT.md) needs : instead of passing on the trade, watch the level on the 5-second chart.

---

## Cautions

- The **win rate is lower** than the GUS, especially with a tight stop — a stock can print several
  penny breaks before it turns.
- They appear **everywhere**. Be selective : pair the signal with a setup you already recognise.
- Prefer the **second test** of a level. A triple top can work, but it may just be the step before the
  next leg up — a triple-top penny break that bounces is one to cut early.
- Move the stop to **break-even** as soon as new rejection levels form on the way down : the trade
  becomes a free play, and a break-even exit keeps you able to take the next one.
- Not ideal on an **SSR** stock : one of the four sellers is missing.
- Fast : on the 5-second chart, execution is close to market.
