# Short Into Resistance — pattern sheet

> **Short** a stock as it reaches a resistance. The bet : the level holds, because someone is waiting
> there to sell.

*Last revised : 2026-09-25, from the Trading Desk session of 14 September, « Les Patterns ».*

---

## Entry checklist

| # | Criterion | Value | Why |
|---|-----------|-------|-----|
| 1 | **A resistance** | A level the market remembers | Daily first ; premarket works too. |
| 2 | **Volume at the level** | Traded there, ideally heavily | The volume is the stuck longs who will sell on the way back (the bag holders — see [`four-sellers.md`](../notes/four-sellers.md)). |
| 3 | **Candles at the level** | Red rather than green | The best resistances were made by selling. |
| 4 | **An extension into it** | The stock runs up to the level | A flat stock sitting at a resistance gives nothing to short. |

---

## No trigger of its own

A short into resistance says **where**, not **when**. It has no entry signal of its own : it resolves
into one of the execution signals, at the level —

- a **double top** on the level ([`DT.md`](DT.md)) ;
- a **penny break** of the level ([`penny-break.md`](penny-break.md)) — « short into resistance plus
  penny break » is a good combination ;
- a **consolidation** under it, the push slowing down.

So the sheet is a checklist for the level, and the entry comes from
[`execution-signals.md`](../notes/execution-signals.md). Size and stop follow the signal taken : tight on a
penny break, wider on a double top — with the stop rule of [`stop-rule.md`](../notes/stop-rule.md).
